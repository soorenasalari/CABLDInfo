Abstract

Anatomical landmark detection in medical images is essential for various clinical and research applications, including disease diagnosis and surgical planning. However, manual landmark annotation is time-consuming and requires significant expertise. Existing deep learning methods often require large amounts of well-annotated data, which are costly to acquire. In this paper, we introduce CABLD, a novel self-supervised framework for 3D brain landmark detection in unlabeled scans with varying contrasts by using only a single reference example. We employ an inter-subject landmark consistency loss with an image registration loss and introduce a 3D contrast augmentation strategy to promote generalization. Additionally, we utilize an adaptive mixed loss function to schedule task contributions for optimal outcomes. Experiments on four diverse MRI datasets demonstrate that CABLD outperforms state-of-the-art methods in mean radial error and success detection rates, providing a robust solution that reduces the need for extensive annotations and generalizes well across contrasts.

Introduction

Brain anatomical landmarks serve as critical reference points for neuro-navigation, disease diagnosis, and registration quality assessment. Manual annotation demands expert knowledge and is time-intensive. While deep learning approaches have accelerated landmark detection, they often rely on large labeled datasets and struggle with varying MRI contrasts. Self-supervised methods address label scarcity but may lack consistent protocols across subjects and contrasts. CABLD bridges these gaps by integrating consistency-based losses with contrast augmentation to yield accurate, protocol-driven landmark predictions using only a single reference atlas.

Methodology

The CABLD framework comprises:

  • Consistency-Regularized Training: Inter-subject and template-consistency losses enforce protocol-driven landmark placement.
  • Contrast Augmentation: 3D random convolutions generate diverse MRI intensities from a mono-modal scan to improve robustness.
  • Adaptive Loss Balancing: A curriculum-inspired mixed loss shifts focus from registration to consistency during training.
  • 3D CNN Landmark Detector: A nine-block 3D CNN with center-of-mass layers predicts landmark coordinates.

Results

CABLD was evaluated on HCP-T1w, OASIS, SNSX, and HCP-T2w datasets against classical and learning-based baselines. Key findings include:

  • HCP-T1w: Lowest MRE of 3.27 ± 2.24 mm; SDR@3mm: 54.5%, @6mm: 93.7%, @9mm: 98.9%.
  • OASIS & SNSX: Consistent performance gains over ANTs, NiftyReg, KeyMorph, and supervised CNNs (p < 0.05).
  • HCP-T2w: MRE of 3.99 ± 2.25 mm, competitive with ANTs; highest SDR@6mm and @9mm without T2w training.

Conclusion

CABLD presents a self-supervised, contrast-agnostic solution for 3D brain landmark detection that requires only a single annotated template. By combining consistency-based regularization and random-convolution augmentation, CABLD achieves state-of-the-art accuracy across multiple MRI contrasts and datasets. This approach reduces annotation burdens and offers robust generalization, paving the way for clinical and large-scale neuroimaging applications.
